How MOSFETs Work

[00 title] Here's a wild fact: your smartphone has billions of these tiny switches inside. Without them, your computer wouldn't exist.
[01 valve] Think of a MOSFET like a water tap. The Source is where the water comes in. The Drain is where it flows out. But the Gate? That's your finger on the knob.
[02 counter] That tap controls a massive flow. A modern chip packs a billion of these babies onto a piece of sand no bigger than your fingernail.
[03 depth] The Gate sits incredibly close to the channel. We're talking atoms away. This tiny gap is what makes or breaks the switch.
[04 silicon] The whole thing sits on a Silicon crystal. Pure silicon doesn't conduct well, so we dope it with Phosphorus or Boron to create paths for electricity.
[05 mosfet] When no voltage hits the Gate, the channel stays closed. It's like a dry riverbed. No water flows from Source to Drain. The switch is OFF.
[06 mosfet] Apply a positive voltage to the Gate. It pulls electrons down. A conductive bridge forms between Source and Drain. Water flows. The switch is ON.
[07 facts] So there you have it. Voltage at the Gate opens the path. No voltage, and it closes. Simple, right?
[08 circuit] Hook this up to a battery and an LED. No gate voltage? LED stays dark. Add gate voltage? LED lights up. That's amplification in a nutshell.
[09 counter] It's been about fifty years since the MOSFET was born. It killed the bulky vacuum tube and gave us the digital age.
